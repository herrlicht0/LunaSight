LunaSight quick user guide

Choose a lunar location, inspect its lighting and Earth visibility, and compare candidate sites. The app is an educational planning aid, not a landing approval or flight tool.

1. Choose a place

  1. Select English or Turkish from the Language menu in the upper-right corner.
  2. Choose a globe preset for the near side, far side, east, west, north pole or south pole.
  3. Hold the left mouse button and drag on the Moon to rotate the globe. Dragging does not select a site.
  4. Use the mouse wheel/trackpad or the + and − buttons to zoom. Select Reset view to return to the South Pole preset and default zoom.
  5. Make a short click on the lunar surface to select a location. Quick-site choices and optional manual coordinates are also available.
  6. The selected location stays marked while you rotate. If it is on the far side, a direction arrow at the edge points toward it.

2. Check sunlight and Earth visibility

  1. Choose Set date and check this place to open the date and sky view.
  2. Enter the landing date and time in UTC, then press Check sunlight and Earth with NASA/JPL. An internet connection is needed for live Horizons results.
  3. Review the Sun and Earth elevations and the estimated visibility over the next 24 hours. Open Sunlight & Earth link for plain-language radio and lighting explanations.
  4. The selected-site panel shows an eye-level surface perspective. When an analysis is available, the Sun and Earth are marked if they fall within the view.

3. Compare places

Add candidate locations to the comparison list. Double-click a row to load it into the date and sky view. Export the list when you want to keep a copy.

Terrain and results

The whole-Moon map uses NASA LOLA elevation and an LROC-derived color texture when loaded. Its 81 × 161 anti-aliased display mesh shows more visible detail while preserving the source dataset's actual resolution; it is for selecting locations and broad context, not landing-hazard assessment. Detailed local terrain is available only for selected regions. After analysis, the eye-level perspective faces the Sun and dims when the Sun is below the local horizon. Without NASA elevation, a repeatable site-specific schematic is shown; it is illustrative, not measured terrain or a camera image.

Lighting, visibility and radio outputs are simplified estimates. A promising-geometry label is not a safety assessment, landing-site certification or a mission-specific communications link budget.
